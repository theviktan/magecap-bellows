# MageCap Bellows 0.7.0

Download `MageCap-Bellows-0.7.0-Windows.zip` below, unzip it anywhere and open `START HERE.txt`.
Everything is included (Python too); you need a graphics driver with Vulkan 1.3.

These notes grow as 0.7.0's work lands; builds until the release report `0.7.0-dev`.

## Free and open source

## A new look, step one

- The editor takes on MageCap's navy colors, shared with MageCap Forge, with rounder fields and tabs.
- **Orange now means "selected" (and Play).** Toggles, check marks, folders, graphs and dialog
  buttons are neutral, so the selection stands out; a hovered row is a neutral highlight that never
  looks selected. ForgeScript's script badge is green, its own color.
- The title bar says **MageCap Bellows**. More of the new design (a title bar with search, an icon
  rail, a floating viewport toolbar, a new Inspector) follows in later steps.

## Hierarchy groups, part one

- Scenes can have **groups**: named folders in the Hierarchy, each with an icon, a color and a note,
  that top-level entities are filed under (children follow their parent). A group never moves or
  parents anything. Groups are saved with the scene and kept in play mode, and a scene change keeps
  the groups of entities kept across scenes.
- Scripts can use them, in ForgeScript and Python alike: `group(name)`, `groups()` and
  `create_group(name, icon)`; a group's `entities()`, `all_entities()`, `has_entity`, `add_entity`,
  `remove_entity` and `delete()`; and an entity's `group` and `is_in_group(name)`.
- ForgeScript also has phrases for them: `everything in group "Docking"`, `everything under group
  "Docking"`, `group "Docking" has bay`, `put bay in group "Docking"` and `remove bay from group
  "Docking"`. A misspelled group name is an error that names the closest group.
- **In the Hierarchy:** the folder button next to the search box makes a group, and opens its
  settings: name, note, one of 16 icons, a color, and Delete Group (its entities stay). Drag an
  entity onto a group, or right-click it → **Move to Group** (also **No Group** and **New
  Group...**; a selection moves together). Each group shows its icon on a tile of its color, how many
  entities it holds and its note, and opens and closes like a node; right-click it to select its
  entities, move it up or down, or delete it. Entities in no group come after a "Not in a group" line,
  and dropping onto that line takes one out. Every change is one undo step.
- **Badges:** gray icons at the end of each row show what else an entity has: physics, sound, a
  light, animation or a nav agent, next to the script badge that opens its script.
- Editor automation can read the panel: `editor.hierarchy_rows()`.

## Hierarchy groups, part three

- **Views:** **Groups** (as before), **By type** (every entity under Scripted, Lights, Cameras, UI,
  Audio, Physics, Meshes or Other) and **By tag** (under each of its tags, then No tags). The flat
  views say which group an entity is in.
- **Search** understands `tag:enemy`, `group:docking` and `has:script` (also `light`, `camera`,
  `physics`, `audio`, `mesh`, `ui`, `animation`, `nav`, or `tag` for any tag), and four chips under
  it (Has script, Lights, Physics, Tagged) narrow the list with one click.
- **Pin to Top** (right-click an entity) lists it under PINNED at the top, wherever it is.
- **Stacks:** three or more siblings in a row with one numbered name (`Crate 1`, `Crate 2`, ... or
  `SM_Flower_57`, `SM_Flower_58`, ...) fold into one row, "Crate ×40". Open it to see each one; click
  it to select them all.
- **Eye and lock** on each group, for the editor only: a hidden group's meshes, lights and world text
  aren't drawn in the viewport, and a locked group's entities can't be clicked there (the Hierarchy
  still selects them). The game always shows them. Scripts see them as `hidden_in_editor` and
  `locked_in_editor`.
- **Organize** (the sparkle button) suggests groups for top-level lights and skies (Lighting),
  cameras (Cameras), UI canvases (UI) and sound sources (Audio) that are in no group, shows every
  move first, and makes them as one undo step when you click Apply. Scripted entities stay put.
- Editor automation: `set_hierarchy_view`, `set_hierarchy_search`, `organize_scene`, `set_pinned`.
- An entity's `tags` are now called labels in the docs, so they aren't confused with groups.

## Profiler

- **View → Profiler** shows where each frame's time goes on the CPU. Every part of the frame is
  timed: reading input and events, the scene update (game UI, animation, tweens, scripts,
  navigation, physics and its fixed-update and contact scripts, audio), drawing the scene and the
  editor's own UI, and waiting for the next image and presenting it, where vsync and a busy GPU
  show.
- **Each Python script gets its own line** under Scripts, named by its file, so a slow script stands
  out. ForgeScript's time shows as one line for now.
- **The graph** shows the last 300 frames, with guides at 60 and 30 FPS. **Average** lists each
  part's total time (with the parts inside it), self time (without them), its slowest frame and
  how often it runs, over the last 30 to 300 frames. **Clicking a bar** pauses recording and shows
  that frame, part by part, in the **Frame** tab; **Pause** and **Clear** are on the toolbar.
- **GPU time, render pass by render pass.** Choose **GPU** on the Profiler's toolbar to see how
  long the graphics card spends on the viewport's shadows, depth prepass, ambient occlusion,
  opaque and transparent objects, sky, bloom, tone mapping, text, game UI and selection outline,
  and on the editor's own UI. The graph draws GPU time as a blue line over the CPU bars, so a
  slow frame shows whether the CPU or the GPU held it up. GPU times arrive a few frames after
  their frame (the GPU finishes its work after the CPU has moved on); a graphics driver that
  can't time the GPU says so.
- The profiler times play inside the editor's viewport; a game played in its own window isn't
  included yet.
- Editor automation can read it: `editor.profiler_frames(count)`, `editor.profiler_summary(frames, gpu)`,
  `editor.set_profiler_paused(paused)`, `editor.profiler_paused()`, `editor.clear_profiler()` and
  `editor.gpu_timing_supported()`.
