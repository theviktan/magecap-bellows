# MageCap Bellows 0.7.0

Download `MageCap-Bellows-0.7.0-Windows.zip` below, unzip it anywhere and open `START HERE.txt`.
Everything is included (Python too); you need a graphics driver with Vulkan 1.3.

These notes grow as 0.7.0's work lands; builds until the release report `0.7.0-dev`.

## Free and open source

- **MageCap Bellows and MageCap ForgeScript are now open source under the Apache License 2.0.** Bellows is
  free; the games you make with it are yours to sell under any license, with no royalties.
- The download includes `LICENSE` and `NOTICE`, and **Help → About** says so. Exported games
  include the engine's license and notice as `Bellows-LICENSE.txt` and `Bellows-NOTICE.txt`, next
  to `ThirdPartyNotices.md`.

## A new look, step one

- The editor takes on MageCap's navy colors, shared with MageCap Forge, with rounder fields and tabs.
- **Orange now means "selected" (and Play).** Toggles, check marks, folders, graphs and dialog
  buttons are neutral, so the selection stands out; a hovered row is a neutral highlight that never
  looks selected. ForgeScript's script badge is green, its own color.
- The title bar says **MageCap Bellows**.

## A new look, step two: the title bar and the rail

- **One title bar** holds the menus, the search, the project and scene you're in (a dot means
  unsaved changes), **Play** (the one orange button: it turns into Stop while the game runs, with
  where it plays, Pause and Step beside it) and **Export game**. The old second toolbar row is gone,
  so the panels get its space, and a thin orange line under the bar shows that the game is running.
- **Search (Ctrl+K, or click the box):** type a few letters to find any command (Save Scene, Play,
  Undo, Show Grid, Reset Layout...), any entity (it's selected and framed), any scene (it opens) or
  any script (it opens in the Script workspace). Letters may be scattered: "sv sc" finds Save Scene.
  Up and Down choose, Enter runs, Esc closes; a command that can't run now is listed dimmed.
- **The rail** down the left switches between the **Scene** and **Script** workspaces, and shows or
  hides the **Profiler** and the **Content** panel; **Settings** sits at the bottom.
- Editor automation: `search`, `run_command`, `open_search`, `close_search`.

## A new look, step three: the panels

- **Panels are rounded cards** with gaps between them and around them, on the darker background.
  Drag a gap to resize the panels on either side (a small grip marks each one).
- **Panel tabs are pills:** the open one is raised, the others flat until you point at them, and a
  panel's tab row is part of its card.
- **Selections are warm:** the selected entity in the Hierarchy, the open folder in Content and the
  selected asset have an orange fill, a thin orange outline and warm text, so a selection never
  looks like the plain highlight under the mouse.

## A new look, step four: the viewport

- **One floating toolbar** at the top center of the viewport holds the tools (Select, Move,
  Rotate, Scale), **snapping**, **World / Local** and **Play** with where it plays, Pause and Step.
  Centered, it keeps clear of a game UI anchored to a corner. Play stays in the title bar only while
  the viewport is hidden (the Script workspace).
- **Snapping:** click the magnet to snap all the time (hold Ctrl to move freely); off, hold Ctrl
  to snap, as before. It shows the step for the tool in hand (0.5 m, 15 deg, x 0.1); right-click it
  to set the three steps. The editor remembers them.
- **The orientation gizmo** at the top right shows which way X, Y and Z point; click an axis to look
  along it.
- **Along the bottom:** the camera's speed, and the frame rate with the CPU and GPU time of each
  frame (green at 55 FPS and up, yellow from 30, red below).
- **A faint glow** around the selection's outline; **View → Selection Glow** turns it off.
- Still to come: the new Inspector.

## Hierarchy groups, part one

- Scenes can have **groups**: named folders in the Hierarchy, each with an icon, a color and a note,
  that top-level entities are filed under (children follow their parent). A group never moves or
  parents anything. Groups are saved with the scene and kept in play mode, and a scene change keeps
  the groups of entities kept across scenes.
- Scripts can use them, in ForgeScript and Python alike: `group(name)`, `groups()` and
  `create_group(name, icon)`; a group's `entities()`, `all_entities()`, `has_entity`, `add_entity`,
  `remove_entity` and `delete()`; and an entity's `group` and `is_in_group(name)`.
- ForgeScript also has phrases for them: `everything in group "Docking"`, `everything under group
  "Docking"`, `group "Docking" has bay`, `put bay in group "Docking"` and `remove bay from group
  "Docking"`. A misspelled group name is an error that names the closest group.
- **In the Hierarchy:** the folder button next to the search box makes a group, and opens its
  settings: name, note, one of 16 icons, a color, and Delete Group (its entities stay). Drag an
  entity onto a group, or right-click it → **Move to Group** (also **No Group** and **New
  Group...**; a selection moves together). Each group shows its icon on a tile of its color, how many
  entities it holds and its note, and opens and closes like a node; right-click it to select its
  entities, move it up or down, or delete it. Entities in no group come after a "Not in a group" line,
  and dropping onto that line takes one out. Every change is one undo step.
- **Badges:** gray icons at the end of each row show what else an entity has: physics, sound, a
  light, animation or a nav agent, next to the script badge that opens its script.
- Editor automation can read the panel: `editor.hierarchy_rows()`.

## Hierarchy groups, part three

- **Views:** **Groups** (as before), **By type** (every entity under Scripted, Lights, Cameras, UI,
  Audio, Physics, Meshes or Other) and **By tag** (under each of its tags, then No tags). The flat
  views say which group an entity is in.
- **Search** understands `tag:enemy`, `group:docking` and `has:script` (also `light`, `camera`,
  `physics`, `audio`, `mesh`, `ui`, `animation`, `nav`, `particles`, `decal`, or `tag` for any tag), and four chips under
  it (Has script, Lights, Physics, Tagged) narrow the list with one click.
- **Pin to Top** (right-click an entity) lists it under PINNED at the top, wherever it is.
- **Stacks:** three or more siblings in a row with one numbered name (`Crate 1`, `Crate 2`, ... or
  `SM_Flower_57`, `SM_Flower_58`, ...) fold into one row, "Crate ×40". Open it to see each one; click
  it to select them all.
- **Eye and lock** on each group, for the editor only: a hidden group's meshes, lights and world text
  aren't drawn in the viewport, and a locked group's entities can't be clicked there (the Hierarchy
  still selects them). The game always shows them. Scripts see them as `hidden_in_editor` and
  `locked_in_editor`.
- **Organize** (the sparkle button) suggests groups for top-level lights and skies (Lighting),
  cameras (Cameras), UI canvases (UI) and sound sources (Audio) that are in no group, shows every
  move first, and makes them as one undo step when you click Apply. Scripted entities stay put.
- Editor automation: `set_hierarchy_view`, `set_hierarchy_search`, `organize_scene`, `set_pinned`.
- An entity's `tags` are now called labels in the docs, so they aren't confused with groups.

## Particles

- **A new Particle Emitter component** (Add Component → Rendering, or Create → Particles) sends
  camera-facing particles from the entity: fire, sparks, smoke, dust, magic.
- **Emission:** a rate per second, a burst at the start of each cycle, a cycle length, looping or
  one shot, and a cap on how many live at once (up to 10,000 per emitter).
- **Motion:** lifetime and speed, each with a variation; a cone of directions around the entity's
  up; born at a point, in a sphere or in a box; gravity (or negative gravity, so smoke rises) and
  drag; in world space (trails stay behind a moving entity) or the entity's own space.
- **Look:** size and color from birth to death (fade them out with an end alpha of 0), an intensity
  that glows and blooms above 1, Additive (fire, sparks) or Alpha (smoke, dust) blending, and an
  optional image; without one each particle is a soft round dot. Particles hide behind walls and
  are drawn before bloom, so bright ones glow.
- **They play in the editor too,** so you see the effect while you tune it; **Restart** in the
  Inspector replays it, and play always starts every emitter afresh. A hidden group's emitters
  aren't drawn in the viewport. The Statistics panel counts the particles drawn.
- **Scripts:** the `ParticleEmitter` component, with `play()`, `stop(clear)`, `emit(count)`,
  `is_playing` and `particle_count`, from Python and MageCap ForgeScript. ForgeScript also has
  phrases: `play particles on campfire`, `pause particles on campfire` (no new ones), `clear
  particles on campfire`, `emit 30 particles from sparks` and `the particles on smoke are playing`.

## Decals

- **A new Decal component** (Add Component → Rendering, or Create → Decal) projects an image onto
  the surfaces inside its box: footprints, cracks, scorch marks, posters, puddles, road paint.
- **Lit like the surface:** a decal changes the surface's color before lighting, so it's shaded,
  shadowed and lit by every light just as the floor or wall under it is.
- **Placing one:** move, turn and scale the entity like any other; the box (green when selected)
  projects down the entity's -Y. **Size** sets the image's width and height and how deep the box
  reaches; **Max Angle** fades it out on surfaces turned away (so a floor decal doesn't smear down
  a wall); **Color** tints it and sets its opacity; **Sort Order** puts one decal over another.
  Without an image, a decal is a plain rectangle of color.
- Up to 64 decals in view at once, with up to 16 different images. The Statistics panel counts
  them. Scripts use the `Decal` component from Python and MageCap ForgeScript, and ForgeScript can
  paint one in a line: `paint "Textures/Scorch.png" at hit` puts a decal where a raycast hit,
  facing the surface (`sized 0.4` for its size).

## Typed script settings in the Inspector

- A ForgeScript setting written with a type, like `set speed as number to 5`, gets a decimal field
  in the Inspector even though 5 is whole; without a type, `set lives to 5` stays a whole-number
  field, as before.

## Profiler

- **View → Profiler** shows where each frame's time goes on the CPU. Every part of the frame is
  timed: reading input and events, the scene update (game UI, animation, tweens, scripts,
  navigation, physics and its fixed-update and contact scripts, audio), drawing the scene and the
  editor's own UI, and waiting for the next image and presenting it, where vsync and a busy GPU
  show.
- **Each Python script gets its own line** under Scripts, named by its file, so a slow script stands
  out. ForgeScript's time shows as one line for now.
- **The graph** shows the last 300 frames, with guides at 60 and 30 FPS. **Average** lists each
  part's total time (with the parts inside it), self time (without them), its slowest frame and
  how often it runs, over the last 30 to 300 frames. **Clicking a bar** pauses recording and shows
  that frame, part by part, in the **Frame** tab; **Pause** and **Clear** are on the toolbar.
- **GPU time, render pass by render pass.** Choose **GPU** on the Profiler's toolbar to see how
  long the graphics card spends on the viewport's shadows, depth prepass, ambient occlusion,
  opaque and transparent objects, sky, bloom, tone mapping, text, game UI and selection outline,
  and on the editor's own UI. The graph draws GPU time as a blue line over the CPU bars, so a
  slow frame shows whether the CPU or the GPU held it up. GPU times arrive a few frames after
  their frame (the GPU finishes its work after the CPU has moved on); a graphics driver that
  can't time the GPU says so.
- The profiler times play inside the editor's viewport; a game played in its own window isn't
  included yet.
- Editor automation can read it: `editor.profiler_frames(count)`, `editor.profiler_summary(frames, gpu)`,
  `editor.set_profiler_paused(paused)`, `editor.profiler_paused()`, `editor.clear_profiler()` and
  `editor.gpu_timing_supported()`.
