# MageCap Bellows 0.6.0

Download `MageCap-Bellows-0.6.0-Windows.zip` below, unzip it anywhere and open `START HERE.txt`.
Everything is included (Python too); you need a graphics driver with Vulkan 1.3.

These notes grow as 0.6.0's work lands; builds until the release report `0.6.0-dev`.

## Tweens

- **`tween(entity, property, to, seconds, ease, delay)`** moves a property smoothly over time, from
  where it is to where you say: a door swinging open, a menu sliding in, a light dimming, music
  fading out. **`tween_by`** adds an amount instead (a wheel can spin twice), and **`stop_tweens`**
  stops an entity's tweens.
- **Any number or vector property** can be tweened, named as scripts name it: an entity's
  `position`, `rotation` and `scale`, or a component's, such as `"UIImage.color"`,
  `"UIRect.position"` or `"AudioSource.volume"`. Rotations turn the shortest way.
- **The same 11 easing styles as ForgeScript's `ease()`:** `"linear"`, `"in"`, `"out"`,
  `"in out"`, the `"sine"` and `"back"` styles, `"bounce out"` and `"elastic out"`.
- **A `Tween` handle** repeats (`repeat`, forever too), goes back and forth (`yoyo`), keeps moving
  while the game is paused (`while_paused`), and can be paused, resumed, stopped or completed. In
  ForgeScript, `wait until t.finished` waits for it.
- **`tween finished`** goes to every script when a tween reaches its end, with the entity, the
  property and the tween's id.
- A new tween of a property replaces the one already moving it, so tweens never fight. Tweens of
  entities kept across scenes keep going through `load_scene`.
- **In ForgeScript, tweens read as English:** `move door to (0, 0, 5) over 2 seconds`,
  `turn wheel by (0, 360, 0) over 1 second`, `scale coin to 2 over 0.3 seconds`, and
  `fade title to 0 over 1 second`, with `with "bounce out"` on the end for another easing style.
  Each gives back the `Tween`. `push ball by (0, 5, 0)` gives a body a sudden push.
- Python's `emit` (and saved data) now take `Vec2` and `Vec4` values too; they arrive as lists of
  numbers, the way ForgeScript writes them.

## Tags

- **Entities can have tags,** like `"enemy"` or `"pickup"`, set in the Inspector under the entity's
  name (type one and press Enter; click one to remove it) and saved with the scene and in prefabs.
- **Scripts find a group at once:** `find_tagged("enemy")`, and in ForgeScript
  `for each enemy in everything tagged "enemy"`. `entity.tags`, `add_tag`, `remove_tag` and
  `has_tag` work in both languages, and ForgeScript also reads `tag door as "locked"` and
  `untag door as "locked"`.

## Skeletal animation

- **Rigged glTF models come in with their skeleton and animations,** up to 256 bones and 256 clips
  each (morph targets aren't supported yet). Linear, stepped and smooth (cubic) keyframes all play
  as made.
- **The Animator component** plays the model's clips: pick the clip in the Inspector, with Play On
  Start, Loop and a Speed from -10 to 10 (negative plays it backward). It runs in game time, so
  pausing the game pauses it.
- **Preview** plays the clip in the viewport while you edit, and its Time slider shows any moment
  of it. Nothing it does is saved or added to undo.
- **Scripts play clips** with `animator.play("Run")`, crossfading from the pose on screen (0.2
  seconds unless you say otherwise), and can `stop`, `pause` and `resume`, change `speed`, `loop`
  and `time`, and read `clip`, `playing`, `length`, `clips` and `bones`. `bone_position` and
  `bone_rotation` tell where a bone is this frame, to put something in a hand. A clip that doesn't
  loop sends **`animation finished`** when it ends.
- **In ForgeScript:** `play animation "Walk" on hero`, `pause animation on hero` and
  `resume animation on hero`, and `when animation finished with who, clip`.
- Models are skinned on the graphics card, crossfade smoothly between clips, and cast shadows in
  their pose. Culling follows the pose too, so a swinging arm never disappears at the
  edge of the screen.

## Navigation

- **Scenes get a navmesh:** what agents can walk on, baked from the static colliders in **Scene
  Settings → Navigation** for an agent's size, step height and steepest slope. It's saved beside the
  scene and ships with the game, and the panel says when it's out of date.
- **Scripts find paths:** `find_path(start, end)` gives a path's corners around walls and obstacles,
  `nearest_navigable_point` and `is_navigable` check where agents can stand, and `bake_navigation()`
  rebuilds it for worlds made while the game runs.
- Big worlds are baked in 32 m tiles, up to 4 km across. Navmesh files are fully checked when they
  load, so a damaged one is refused with a reason.

## Camera shake

- **`shake_camera(strength, seconds)`** shakes the game camera's view for a hit, an explosion or a
  landing, fading out on its own. ForgeScript reads `shake the camera by 0.5 over 0.3 seconds`.
  Only the picture moves, so the camera's position, physics and aiming aren't thrown off, and a
  paused game holds the shake until it goes on.

## Data files

- **`load_json(path)` and `load_text(path)`** read game data from the Assets folder (item lists,
  dialogue, level tables), and ForgeScript reads `set items to the data in "Data/items.json"`. The
  files ship with the game, are read again only when they change, and can't reach outside Assets.
  A mistake in a JSON file gives its line and column.

## Editor fixes

- **No more console windows on Windows.** The editor and exported games are now Windows GUI
  programs, so starting either never opens a console window beside it. The log is still in the
  Console panel and the log file; `--console` shows it in a console window when you want one.
- **Numbers that aren't numbers are refused.** Typing `nan` or `inf` into an Inspector or Settings
  field keeps the old value and says why. A scene file with a value that can't be used (`null`, the
  wrong kind, or too big) loads with that value's default, and the Console names the entity and
  field, so the editor and the game always agree. Scripts get the same rule: a position, rotation,
  scale or any other vector or color with NaN or infinity in it is refused with an error that names
  it, in Python and ForgeScript.
- **The Hierarchy only draws the rows you can see,** so a scene with 20,000 entities stays smooth.
- **Python that never finishes no longer freezes the editor.** In the editor, a callback, or code
  typed in the Console, that runs over 10 seconds is stopped with `KeyboardInterrupt`, as
  ForgeScript stops a script that runs too long, even when the code catches everything with
  `except:`. Shipped games have no limit, so a long load is never cut off, and
  `bellows.set_python_time_limit(seconds)` lets a game choose its own.
- **Windows device names** (`CON`, `NUL`, `COM1` and the like) are refused as project names and in
  New Script paths, and an exported game is never named after one. Accented letters in a project
  name become plain ones in its folder ("Tëst" makes `Test`) instead of disappearing, and the
  project file always lands in the project's own folder, whatever the name holds.
- **Smaller fixes:** emoji typed into names are kept, and drawn with the system's emoji font where
  there is one; vector fields that are too narrow for their values stack instead of cutting numbers
  off; a Python error counts once in the Console, not once per traceback line; a refused asset
  rename says why in its dialog; and the Game menu's game-window option reads
  **Use a Game Window for Play**.

## ForgeScript 1.7.0

Faster scripts, a way to see where their time goes, dates, and two crash fixes. The one thing a
1.6 script sees differently is how a list that holds itself is written out (see Safer).

**Faster**
- **Functions passed to built-ins run about ten times faster:** `map`, `filter`, `sort_by`, `max_by`
  and every other built-in that calls one of your functions no longer sets up a new task for each
  item. The closures benchmark (`map` and `filter` over 20,000 numbers, five times) went from 147 ms
  to 15 ms.

**Finding what's slow**
- **`forgescript --profile game.forge`** runs the script, then lists the functions that did the most
  work, with how often each was called and where it is. `--output profile.txt` writes the list to a
  file instead. The counts are the same on every computer, so a profile from a player's machine
  matches yours.

**Dates and the real clock**
- **`date()`** gives today's year, month, day, hour, weekday and more as a map, in the player's time
  zone, and `date(moment)` does the same for a moment you saved with `real_time()`.
  `format_date(moment, "Weekday, Month DD")` writes one as text, and `days_between(from, to)`
  counts calendar days, so a daily reward or a streak knows when a new day began, even across
  midnight. `utc_date` is the same in UTC, for answers that don't depend on where the player is.

**Safer**
- **Runaway text and lists no longer close the game or the editor.** A loop like
  `set s to s + s` used to grow until memory ran out and the program shut down. Now joining text
  stops at 100 million bytes with a clear mistake, and a list that can't grow any more gives an
  error your script can catch with `try`.
- **A list that holds itself no longer freezes the game or the editor.** If a list (or map, or
  object) is added to itself, `say` and `"{...}"` now show `[...]` where it comes back, as in
  `[1, [...]]`. Before, such a list could freeze the game for half a minute or close it, and
  stopping at a breakpoint near one could hang the editor's Variables panel.
